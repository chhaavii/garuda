The gap

The warning exists. It just never reaches the household.

01

Satellites see it first

VIIRS and MODIS thermal anomalies flag a hotspot within minutes. That data is free — and almost nobody downstream reads it.

02

Weather and terrain make it worse

Dry fine fuel, wind, an upslope gradient. The same fire is a nuisance upwind and an emergency downwind.

03

Nobody tells the household

No SMS. No voice note. No LED on the wall. The last mile is the missing product, not the missing satellite.

How it thinks

01 / detect

A pixel heats up

FIRMS reports a thermal anomaly with a fire radiative power value. We drop low-confidence pixels and keep everything inside the 25 km watch ring.

02 / condition

Then the weather answers

Wind speed and direction, humidity, vapour-pressure deficit, heating rate, slope and fuel class. Wind blowing upslope multiplies the spread estimate.

→→→→

03 / decide

27 fuzzy rules fire

Mamdani inference, centroid defuzzification. Each rule reports its strength, so "why HIGH?" always has an answer.

IF ffmc extreme AND wind strong → HIGH IF fire near AND downwind → HIGH IF ffmc low AND air humid → LOW

04 / speak

Plain language, not a dashboard

A number an operator can audit, and a sentence a resident can act on — in the same cycle.

GARUDAHIGH fire risk: fire within 1.2 km, strong wind. Evacuate now.

05 / deliver

Four channels, one message

Discord for the ops room, SMS for the feature phone, email for the record, an LED on the wall for the street.

    Honest limits

    What this does not do.

    • Live vs simulated. Weather, terrain and air quality are live Open-Meteo data. NASA FIRMS needs a MAP_KEY; without it the map and detections are visibly badged SIMULATED.
    • FIRMS lags. Satellite overpasses mean detection can arrive up to about three hours after ignition, and cloud cover hides fires.
    • Rules, not guarantees. The fuzzy engine is expert heuristics. It ranks risk; it cannot promise a fire will or will not reach a street.
    • No oxygen prediction. Outdoor O₂ stays near 20.9%. A sensor reading is context for responders, not a predictor, and never changes the score.
    • Background is illustrative. The terrain, embers, satellite pass and heat map behind this page are decorative, generated from noise — not this geofence.
